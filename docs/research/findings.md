# Findings

One row per finding, assembled from the three track documents. Marks per [README.md](README.md).

**This is a register, not a plan.** The "would touch" column names what a future decision would have to
consider — it does not propose that decision.

A survey that only confirms is not a survey. These are the ones that disagree with us.

## 2. Standardised already — we reinvented it

| #   | Finding                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                       | Mark                                                 | Would touch                                                                                                                                                        | Confidence |
| --- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ---------- |
| F5  | **`req_permissions` is a field in the W3C MiniApp Manifest spec**, alongside `app_id`, `version`, `platform_version`, `device_type`, `pages`, `widgets`, `color_scheme`, `window`. Our `capabilities` field is a reinvented standard field, and `contractVersion`/`minimumHostVersion` are near-copies of `platform_version`. | Documented                                           | Our pack manifest; [ADR-0001](https://github.com/mr-rogers-neighborhood/suite-go/blob/main/docs/adr/0001-packs-declare-capabilities.md); `docs/reference/packs.md` | High       |
| F6  | **MiniApp packages are ZIP archives with a manifest at the root**, and the Packaging spec defines the container formally. Ours is the same shape, designed independently.                                                                                                                                                     | Documented                                           | `pack.mjs` and the pack format docs — adopt the spec's field names or record why ours differ                                                                       | High       |
| F7  | **A MiniApp URI scheme exists as a spec** (prefix + `miniapp` infix + identifier + version), explicitly to stop every vendor inventing one.                                                                                                                                                                                   | Documented                                           | `suite://` — probably not adopt, but the _existence_ of the spec should be recorded as a decision not to                                                           | Medium     |
| F8  | **Widgets and multi-entry are standardised in intent** — a widget is "a special form of MiniApp Page" that can occupy part of the screen; "single-instance, multi-entries" is a named core feature. We deferred both.                                                                                                         | Documented (definitions); **Unverified** (mechanics) | The deferred widget and per-app launcher-entry work                                                                                                                | Medium     |

**F5 is the sharpest thing in this report.** We wrote a capability model, an ADR about it, and an
enforcement path — and a standard field with the same job has existed the whole time. That is not a defect
in the implementation; it is a defect in method, and the fix is a habit rather than a change.

## What this register suggests about our method

Three findings (F5, F6, F21) are things a competitor or a spec already did, discovered after we had built
and documented our own version. None of them is a defect in the work; all three are the same defect in
method: **we designed before we surveyed**, because surveying felt slower than deciding.

The correction is cheap and does not need a process: before writing an ADR that standardises a format, spend
twenty minutes checking whether a spec already covers it. Twenty minutes would have found `req_permissions`.

The one thing this report recommends — as a habit, not a decision — is that any future ADR which _defines a
format_ cites either the specification it follows or the search that failed to find one.

## What to do next, in order of cost

Cheapest first, because all of these are optional and the order is the whole point:

1. **Decide about tier 2 (F24).** We have declaration and refusal, and no moment where a person is asked or
   can say no. That is the largest gap in the capability model and the cheapest to notice now.
2. **Write down the mergeable-container rule (F1).** The code is right in all three apps and nothing says
   why, so the next patch that uses a plain `set` reintroduces a data-loss bug silently. A paragraph and a
   test, not a refactor.
3. **Read the W3C Manifest and Packaging specs** (F5, F6). A few hours, and it either validates our format
   or improves it.
4. **Ask about F22.** Whether the first user is told that blindness means no recovery is a question with a
   person as the answer, not a document.
5. **Close G7 and G8.** Both are one reading session and both feed decisions already recorded.
6. **Leave G1, G4, G5, G6, G9, G10 alone** until something depends on them. A gap that blocks nothing is
   not urgent, and this list exists so it is not forgotten rather than so it is filled.
